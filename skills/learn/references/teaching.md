# Explaining a topic

The goal is that the learner could explain the topic to a classmate, not that they have read a summary. Explain in small steps and make them think at each one.

## Before you start

- Read the topic's notes. Teach what is there.
- Check the level (`learner.education_level`) and the goal (`exam.goal`). `pass` means the essentials, clearly. `master` means the edge cases and the why behind the why.
- Ask one quick question to see what they already know ("Have you seen Gauss's law before? What do you remember?"). Start from their answer.

## The explanation, in order

1. **Where it fits.** One or two sentences: what problem this topic solves and how it connects to what they already studied.
2. **The rule, precisely.** State the definition, law or method exactly as the materials do, before any analogy. Precision first: an analogy on top of a vague rule produces confident wrong answers.
3. **An analogy or picture.** Something from everyday life, a small table, or a text diagram. Say where the analogy stops working.
4. **A worked example from the materials.** Go step by step and say why each step is taken. Cite it: `[Tema 1, p. 9]`.
5. **Typical mistakes.** The two or three errors students make with this topic, and how to avoid them.
6. **Check.** One question that requires applying the idea, not repeating it. Wait for the answer before continuing.

## Make it interactive

- **Small pieces.** Three to six short paragraphs, then stop and ask something. Never a wall of text.
- **Ask before telling.** "What do you think happens to the force if the distance doubles?" Then confirm or correct.
- **Let them do the next step.** In a worked example, do the first step and ask for the second.
- **Use structure when it helps.** A table to compare two concepts, a numbered list for a procedure, a text diagram for a process or a hierarchy.
- **Offer another route.** If they hesitate or say they do not get it, explain it differently (another analogy, a simpler case, a concrete number) rather than repeating the same words more slowly.
- **Let them steer.** They can always ask for more depth, an extra example, or to skip what they already know.

## Close the topic

- Ask them to summarise it in their own words in two or three sentences, and correct gently.
- Give a three-line recap: the rule, when to use it, the mistake to avoid.
- Then go to practice (step 3 of `study.md`).

## Honesty

- If the notes do not cover something they ask, say so, then answer from general knowledge and mark it as such.
- If the materials seem to contain an error, point it out and explain; do not silently correct it.
- If you are not sure, say so. A confident wrong explanation costs marks in the exam.
